Why these statistics matter

Employee experience now influences attraction, retention, engagement, productivity, wellbeing, and how people judge an employer’s culture. These numbers show that EX is no longer a side initiative. It is a business issue with measurable workforce and financial impact.

How to use this page

Use the data below to shape HR priorities, strengthen business cases, support leadership discussions, and identify where experience design needs the most attention.

Flexibility remains one of the strongest experience levers available to employers.

20

Autonomy and reduced commuting time can improve how people experience work and output.

Source: Owl Labs

HR focus

What HR teams should take from this

Strategic implications

  • Experience directly affects attraction, retention, engagement, and employer reputation.
  • Flexible work and process design have become central parts of the employee value proposition.
  • Belonging, transparency, and manager quality shape whether people stay and perform well.
  • Employee wellbeing is deeply connected to operational design, not only personal resilience.

Practical priorities

  • Measure experience continuously instead of relying on one annual survey.
  • Fix friction in workflows that create unnecessary stress and wasted effort.
  • Train managers to lead hybrid teams with stronger communication and career support.
  • Design culture, belonging, and fairness as visible employee experiences, not slogans.

Suggested action checklist

  • Audit the top moments that shape your employee experience.
  • Review retention, engagement, burnout, and internal mobility together.
  • Check whether hybrid work is helping or hurting connection and growth.
  • Build clearer communication around compensation, culture, and flexibility.
  • Use listening data to prioritize actions with the highest employee and business value.
