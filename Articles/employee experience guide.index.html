Section 01

What is Employee Experience?

01

Employee experience (EX) is how employees feel about everything they encounter throughout their entire employee journey — from the moment they apply for a role to long after they leave an organisation.

The entirety of employees' material and relational interactions shapes their perception of the organisation. Employee experience is the HR equivalent of customer experience — an "outside-in" approach in which the employee holds a central role in workplace and people-strategy decisions.

Just as selling great products requires empathising with customers, creating a great workplace requires empathising with employees. Employees are the organisation's internal customers. Understanding how situations come across to them helps leaders define problems and craft meaningful solutions.

Customer Experience
Outside-In

Product and marketing decisions centre on what customers want and need, building empathy for the buyer's journey.

Employee Experience
People-First

HR and leadership decisions centre on what employees need, expect, and fear, building a better daily work reality.

The goal is to design an experience that demonstrates genuine care for people within the context of their work. This employee-centric approach fits the modern workplace and enables true empowerment and engagement. Companies with a great employee experience provide a streamlined work environment, enabling people to do their best work and push the company forward.

Section 02

The Three Key Components of Employee Experience

02

While countless factors shape how employees perceive their employer, three interconnected spheres of influence consistently define the overall quality of the experience.

Physical Experience

The sensory elements of the work environment — floor-plan efficiency, desk ergonomics, acoustics, natural light, break spaces, and whether the office layout energises or drains people.

Physical conditions directly affect employee wellbeing, focus, and ability to perform. Thoughtful space design can signal organisational values and boost creativity. For remote or hybrid workers, the organisation rethinks the office as a collaboration hub rather than an individual-work destination.

Digital Experience

Technology is central to almost every job. Whether digital tools meet employee expectations — or frustrate them — has a direct impact on satisfaction and productivity.

Level 1

Fits design purpose but was not built with the user in mind. Often requires duplicate data entry.

Level 2

Appealing and intuitive — once learned it becomes second nature, with self-explanatory new features.

Level 3 — Target

Offers genuine end-user value. Functions well, simple to use, and solves a real problem. This is where organisations must aim.

Cultural Experience

Culture is the personality of an organisation — the values, symbols, and visible behaviours displayed in everyday practices. It's the intangible that determines how work feels.

Without the right culture, even excellent physical spaces and digital tools may fail to create a compelling overall experience. A culture of creativity, collaboration, and empowerment cultivates employees who take risks, innovate, and then work together to implement their best ideas.

Section 04

How to Improve Employee Experience: 10 Best Practices

04

A better employee experience starts with intention. Here are ten evidence-backed practices that HR leaders can adopt to meaningfully improve how employees feel at every stage of their journey.

01

Apply Employee Experience Design Methodology

Approach EX as a design problem. Keep the employee perspective at the forefront by asking: What does this experience mean to the employee? Who is the specific employee consuming this experience? What are the key touchpoints and how will employees interact with them? How do we best facilitate those interactions?

02

Focus on the Full Employee Life Cycle

EX encompasses every stage: Attraction, Recruitment, Onboarding, Retention, Development, Offboarding, and supporting Happy Leavers. Neglecting even one stage ripples negatively through all the others. Build an EX strategy that spans every phase.

03

Foster Total Wellbeing

Employees seek employers who acknowledge they have lives outside work. Supporting physical, mental, financial, and career wellbeing demonstrates that people are valued as human beings, not only as workers — leading to increased retention, engagement, productivity, and reduced absenteeism.

04

Prioritise Digital Employee Experience

Employees expect the same quality of technology at work as they experience in their personal lives. Provide the right tools, plus the training and support to use them effectively. This frees people from frustrating friction and lets them focus on meaningful, high-value work.

05

Collect and Act on Employee Feedback

Go to the source. Feedback mechanisms — including surveys, pulse checks, and Employee Net Promoter Score — show employees that their voice matters. Use this data to measure morale, detect early warning signs of disengagement, and create meaningful action plans.

06

Encourage Employee Autonomy and Decision-Making

Employees who have the freedom to decide how they get work done feel more committed and become more productive. Build trust by allowing experimentation, empowering people to take initiative, placing naturally autonomous individuals in leadership positions, and celebrating results publicly.

07

Commit to DEIB

The unique perspectives of a diverse workforce fuel ingenuity. Invest in an inclusive environment where people believe they belong and have a voice. Promote diversity in hiring and leadership, provide bias training, and create mentorships and employee resource groups that celebrate difference.

08

Develop Strong Leaders and Managers

Leaders and managers have an outsized role in shaping daily employee experience. Authentic, empathetic leaders create positive environments. Equip managers to coach as well as direct — holding meaningful one-on-ones and communicating with genuine concern for individuals.

09

Recognise and Reward Excellence

Employees need a sense of purpose and to know that their contributions matter. Celebrate milestones, promotions, and achievements with private acknowledgments or public events. Reward with bonuses, promotions, and non-monetary incentives. Competitive compensation with meaningful recognition is key to long-term retention.

10

Implement Quick Wins

Not every EX improvement requires a transformation programme. Low-effort, high-impact actions can noticeably shift how people feel about their day-to-day work immediately. See a selection of quick wins below, organised by theme.

Quick Wins to Improve Employee Experience Today
Onboarding & First Impressions

Send a welcome pack before day one. Set up the new hire's workstation and tech in advance. Schedule meaningful intro meetings in the first week.

Manager & Team Connections

Train managers to lead structured one-on-ones. Create peer buddy systems for new joiners. Hold team check-ins that go beyond project status.

Communication & Transparency

Share company decisions before they become rumour. Publish a regular leadership update. Create clear channels for employee questions to leadership.

Work Tools & Support

Audit your most-used tools for usability friction. Provide self-service IT support resources. Offer optional "office hours" for tech troubleshooting.

Culture & Belonging

Introduce a peer recognition channel in Slack or Teams. Celebrate cultural and personal milestones. Ensure all team voices are heard in meetings.

Physical & Digital Space

Survey employees on workspace comfort. Introduce flexible desk options. Ensure remote workers have access to the same resources as office employees.

In understanding what it takes not only to retain employees but to keep them energised and motivated, leaders can curate the employee experience — giving people autonomy and ownership over their professional development.

Janice Burns, Chief People Officer, Degreed
Section 05

Real-World Employee Experience Examples

05

Theory becomes transformation when organisations commit to action. Here are three case studies that demonstrate how tackling physical, digital, and cultural experience challenges delivers measurable results.

Digital Experience

Multinational Consumer Goods Company

A complex IT structure required employees to access multiple systems just to follow up on basic HR queries and cases. The fragmented setup wasted significant time daily across thousands of users.

The organisation replaced their legacy systems with a cloud-based query management platform that consolidated all inquiry tickets in one place, integrated a self-service portal and a chatbot.

✓ Reduced error rates · Saved operational costs · Streamlined employee access to services across HR, IT, and Legal
Cultural Experience

Healthcare Staffing Firm

Despite enviable perks — childcare, a company gym, on-site restaurants — a staff survey revealed employees wanted more consistent and meaningful recognition from their managers and peers.

Leadership launched a standardised recognition and rewards digital platform allowing both manager-to-team and peer-to-peer recognition, plus virtual wellness and team-building activities.

✓ 100% of employees recognised or gave recognition within months · Employee engagement score increased by 6% to 89%
Physical & Digital Experience

Commercial Interiors Provider

A flexible working culture helped this company navigate pandemic lockdowns smoothly. When offices re-opened, they re-imagined the workplace around individual comfort, giving employees choices about how and where they work best.

A new mobile app allowed employees to locate open workspaces throughout the office or stay connected remotely — enabling desk booking, locating amenities, ordering meals, and accessing wellness resources.

✓ Seamless hybrid working · Employees empowered to choose their ideal environment daily

Frequently Asked Questions

06

Quick answers to the most common employee experience questions.

What is employee experience?

+
Employee experience is how employees feel about everything they encounter and observe throughout their employee journey. It relates to the physical, digital, and cultural environments an organisation provides — from the moment a candidate first interacts with the brand to long after they leave.

Why is employee experience important?

+
Research consistently shows that organisations investing in employee experience outperform competitors. They grow faster, pay better, produce more than double the revenue, and are significantly more profitable. Strong EX also makes an employer far more attractive in a competitive talent market, lowering acquisition costs and boosting retention.

What are the three key components of employee experience?

+
The three key components are: (1) Physical experience — the sensory and spatial qualities of the work environment; (2) Digital experience — the technology, tools, and platforms employees use to do their work; and (3) Cultural experience — the values, norms, and interpersonal dynamics that shape how work feels day to day.

How do you improve employee experience?

+
Organisations must take an employee-centric approach to decision-making. This involves applying design thinking to EX initiatives, covering every stage of the employee life cycle, collecting and acting on employee feedback, investing in great leadership and management, fostering total wellbeing, and continuously measuring the impact of EX interventions.

What is the difference between employee experience and employee engagement?

+
Employee engagement measures how committed and motivated employees feel at a given point in time — it is an outcome. Employee experience is the broader umbrella of conditions, systems, culture, and interactions that drive that engagement level. Think of EX as the cause and engagement as one of the effects.

Who is responsible for employee experience?

+
Employee experience is a shared responsibility. HR leads the strategy and creates the frameworks, tools, and programmes. However, every manager shapes the daily experience of their direct reports, and senior leaders set the cultural tone. The best organisations treat EX as a business priority — not just an HR activity.

People First.
Always.

Employees drive business success and customer satisfaction. When employee experience is placed front and centre, people are empowered to do their best work — and the organisation thrives as a result.
