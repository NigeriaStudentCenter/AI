KEY TERMS

Abbreviations to know with AI in HR analytics

The article includes a short glossary covering AI, algorithm, machine learning, generative AI, natural language processing, and chatbot basics. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

AI

Artificial Intelligence

Technology designed to perform tasks by mimicking aspects of human learning, decision-making, and problem-solving.

ALGO

Algorithm

A step-by-step set of instructions a computer follows to complete a task or solve a problem.

ML

Machine Learning

A type of AI that improves with more data and can find patterns and make predictions.

GENAI

Generative AI

AI that creates content such as text, images, audio, or video based on prompts.

NLP

Natural Language Processing

Technology that helps computers understand and work with human language.

BOT

Chatbot

A conversational tool that uses NLP and machine learning to respond to user questions through text or voice.

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

How to use AI in HR analytics: 5 steps

The article’s five-step sequence focuses on privacy and compliance, training HR teams, using AI to complement humans, continuous improvement, and minimizing bias. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

STEP 1

Ensure data privacy and compliance

Collect only necessary data, obtain consent, protect sensitive information, and align with regulatory and security expectations.

Start with trust and control
STEP 2

Train HR teams on AI usage

Provide hands-on training on how AI works, what it can and cannot do, how to interpret outputs, and when to step in.

Capability is part of implementation
STEP 3

Use AI to complement, not replace, humans

Use AI to save time and guide decisions, while keeping personal judgment, context, and accountability with people.

AI should guide, not decide alone
STEP 4

Use AI for continuous improvement

Identify bottlenecks such as candidate drop-off, slow workflows, or unclear patterns and use AI to improve them over time.

Think ongoing optimization
STEP 5

Minimize bias with transparency

Use explainable AI, bias audits, access controls, consent mechanisms, and meaningful human oversight in algorithmic decisions.

Fairness needs active design
