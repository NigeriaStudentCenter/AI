EXTERNAL ENVIRONMENT

Risks outside the organization that HR still needs to manage

The article says external risks are often beyond direct organizational control, but their impact becomes much worse when governance, accountability, and transparency are weak. It specifically highlights reputational issues, legislative challenges, and transparency and explainability concerns. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

EXTERNAL RISK 1

Reputational risk

AI use in hiring, promotion, performance, or employee monitoring can damage trust quickly if people feel decisions are hidden, unfair, or insufficiently human.

  • How will people perceive this action?
  • How will it affect trust in HR?
  • How does sustainability affect employer reputation?
EXTERNAL RISK 2

Legislative risk

As AI regulation expands, especially in people-related processes, HR must keep checking whether AI use complies with evolving legal requirements.

  • Does this use case fall into a high-risk employment area?
  • Are we meeting fairness and transparency requirements?
  • Do we have oversight in place for employment-related AI use?
EXTERNAL RISK 3

Transparency and explainability

If HR cannot explain how AI influences decisions, the organization may face resistance, confusion, and loss of credibility.

  • Can we explain the logic of this tool clearly?
  • Can employees or candidates understand how AI affects them?
  • Is there a clear accountability point for explaining outcomes?
?

Questions to ask

The article suggests asking what people will think, how the action affects sustainability goals, and how it may affect communities and customer perception. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

INTERNAL ENVIRONMENT

Risks inside the organization that HR can influence more directly

The article says internal risks often emerge when skills, governance, and behavioral norms lag behind AI adoption. It highlights ethical considerations, privacy and confidentiality, and bias and fairness as the key internal risk areas. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

INTERNAL RISK 1

Ethical considerations

Organizations need more than legal compliance. They need clear principles about job loss, reskilling, workforce change, and how AI aligns with values and culture.

  • Is this the right decision for the organization?
  • Will this contradict our values?
  • What effect will this have on culture?
INTERNAL RISK 2

Privacy and confidentiality

AI use increases the risk of exposing sensitive employee or organizational information if access, handling, and disclosure controls are weak.

  • What data is being entered into the tool?
  • Who can access outputs and source data?
  • Are confidentiality expectations explicit and enforced?
INTERNAL RISK 3

Bias and fairness

AI systems can reinforce harmful stereotypes or exclude groups unfairly unless model behavior and outcomes are monitored carefully over time.

  • Do we have controls to monitor known bias?
  • Do we oversee how the model continues to learn?
  • How often do we validate performance against intended purpose?

What HR can influence directly

  • Training and AI fluency
  • Guidelines and policy
  • Behavioral expectations
  • Oversight and review routines
  • How AI is embedded into practice

What happens if HR ignores this

  • Well-intentioned AI use can still create unfair outcomes
  • Trust in HR can drop even when tools are technically “working”
  • Behavioral norms can drift faster than formal governance
DATA GOVERNANCE

Data governance is the cornerstone beneath both external and internal risk

Both the article and the uploaded framework position data governance as the supporting foundation for the rest of the model. The article says data governance should cover quality and integrity, collection practices, privacy and security, and lifecycle management. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13} :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}

GOVERNANCE 1

Data quality and integrity

Ensure data is complete, valid, consistent, and accurate enough to support AI use responsibly.

GOVERNANCE 2

Data collection practices

Know where data comes from, how it is labeled, and how bias may enter during collection or preparation.

GOVERNANCE 3

Privacy and security

Use anonymization, encryption, access control, and privacy-led practice wherever appropriate.

GOVERNANCE 4

Lifecycle management

Manage retention, storage, disposal, traceability, and versioning so models and data can be audited.

LEVELS OF MANAGEMENT

Three levels HR should address

The one-pager says organizations and HR need to manage AI-related risk at three levels: policy and philosophy, practice/process/systems, and individual behavior. :contentReference[oaicite:20]{index=20}

LEVEL 1

Policy and philosophy

Set a formal AI policy that explains the organization’s overall governance approach and guides decision-making across all AI adoption.

LEVEL 2

Practice, process, and systems

Control how AI is embedded in workflows, tools, third-party vendor systems, and operating practice so they stay aligned to internal policy.

LEVEL 3

Individual behavior

Train employees, give clear guidelines, and make sure people understand their ethical and legal obligations when using AI tools.
