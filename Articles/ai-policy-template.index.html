PRACTICAL TEMPLATE

A simple AI policy template structure

The article recommends using a template so organizations do not miss critical sections such as purpose, definitions, use rules, human oversight, and review process. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

AI Usage Policy Template Customize for your organization

1. Purpose and scope

Explain why the policy exists and who must follow it.

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and approved third parties using AI tools on behalf of the organization.

2. Definitions

Define what counts as AI, generative AI, machine learning, and covered tools.

For the purpose of this policy, AI tools include generative AI systems, machine learning systems, and approved internal AI assistants.

3. Authorized use guidelines

State which tasks AI may support and where human review is mandatory.

AI may be used for drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, and internal productivity support. It must not be used to finalize hiring decisions, disciplinary outcomes, or external client communications without review.

4. Data handling rules

List restricted data and clarify what cannot be pasted into external systems.

Employees must not enter confidential business information, personally identifiable information, payroll data, medical data, or legally privileged information into unapproved AI tools.

5. Ethical use and bias prevention

Require fairness, transparency, and care in people-impacting use cases.

AI use must align with company values and avoid discriminatory, misleading, or harmful outcomes. High-impact HR use cases require additional review and documented oversight.

6. Human oversight requirements

Clarify where AI supports people and where people remain accountable.

AI outputs are advisory only unless explicitly approved otherwise. Employees remain responsible for checking accuracy, context, legality, and tone before use.

7. Reporting and accountability

Name policy owners and reporting channels for questions or incidents.

Questions or incidents related to AI use must be reported to the designated HR, Legal, Compliance, or IT owner listed in the policy appendix.

8. Training and review cycle

Commit to regular training and scheduled policy updates.

This policy will be reviewed every six months, or sooner if significant regulatory, technological, or operational changes occur.
ROLLOUT PLAN

10 steps to develop and implement a sound AI policy

The article lays out a 10-step approach that starts with current AI use, includes risk assessment and ownership, and ends with review and effectiveness checks. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

STEP 1

Assess current AI usage

Find where teams are already using tools such as generative AI and what risks or gaps already exist.

STEP 2

Consult key departments

Involve HR, legal, compliance, IT, data security, and relevant business stakeholders early.

STEP 3

Use a policy template

Start from a structured template so you do not miss definitions, rules, controls, or ownership.

STEP 4

Conduct a risk assessment

Review legal, ethical, reputational, privacy, and cybersecurity risks before finalizing the policy.

STEP 5

Communicate the policy clearly

Explain what the policy is, why it matters, and how it protects both employees and the organization.

STEP 6

Monitor and update regularly

Review use patterns, feedback, and tool changes at defined intervals such as quarterly or biannually.

STEP 7

Assign policy ownership

Name one or more owners who answer questions, oversee updates, and keep application consistent.

STEP 8

Integrate with existing policies

Align AI rules with data protection, code of conduct, privacy, and IT security policies.

STEP 9

Train employees

Provide practical guidance so staff understand how to use AI safely and effectively within the rules.

STEP 10

Evaluate policy effectiveness

Use audits, feedback, surveys, and compliance checks to see whether the policy is working as intended.
