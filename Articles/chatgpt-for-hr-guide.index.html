STAGE 1

Talent attraction

Use prompts to create stronger attraction materials and employer-branding ideas.

1

Job descriptions

Draft a role-specific job description with clear responsibilities and qualifications.

Write a job description for a [title] position at an [industry] organization. Include the following responsibilities and qualifications: [A, B, C, etc.].
2

Employer branding on social media

Generate post ideas that communicate company culture and employee sentiment more clearly.

List 5 employer branding post ideas for Instagram and LinkedIn that convey company culture and employee sentiment. Explain how these ideas engage job seekers.
STAGE 2

Recruitment

Use prompts to speed up candidate evaluation materials and hiring communication.

3

Interview questions

Ask for targeted questions plus examples of strong and weak candidate answers.

List 4 interview questions that assess a person’s interpersonal communication skills. For each question, provide example answers that a highly skilled candidate and a low-skilled candidate would give.
4

Job offer template

Create a structured, welcoming offer email draft that can be reviewed and personalized.

Write a professional, welcoming job offer email to a candidate named [name] for a [job title] position at [company name]. Include the start date of [date], a starting salary of [salary], and next steps for accepting the offer.
STAGE 3

Onboarding

Use prompts to structure onboarding tasks, guidance, and new-hire feedback collection.

5

Onboarding checklist

Build a more complete onboarding checklist for hybrid or role-specific onboarding.

Create an onboarding checklist and best practices for onboarding a new [job role] working a hybrid schedule. Include preboarding preparations, first day/week activities, role-specific training path, resources for ongoing support, and strategies for integrating new hires into their team and the company culture.
6

Onboarding survey

Draft a 30-day survey that checks clarity, training quality, manager communication, and improvement opportunities.

Formulate a 10-question onboarding evaluation survey to collect feedback from new hires after 30 days of employment. Use multiple-choice questions to rate clarity of onboarding, quality of training and resources, understanding of duties, communication from managers and team members, and suggestions for improvement.
STAGE 4

Development

Use prompts to explore learning options and support manager coaching.

7

Training resources

Ask for role-relevant learning options and summarized course value.

Find 5 online training programs for a [job title] to learn about [topic] and summarize each course’s offerings and participant reviews.
8

Coaching support

Draft questions a manager can use during a more coaching-based performance review conversation.

Provide 10 questions a manager can pose to take a coaching approach to the performance review of a [job title].
STAGE 5

Retention and engagement

Use prompts to support recognition ideas and employee listening efforts.

9

Employee recognition

Generate non-monetary recognition ideas for different workplace contexts.

Find 7 creative ideas for employee recognition that go beyond monetary rewards. They should be suitable for different work environments, including both remote and in-person teams.
10

Employee engagement survey

Create a stronger engagement survey draft with a mix of multiple-choice and open-ended questions.

Generate 12 employee engagement survey questions that will measure how satisfied, motivated, and connected employees feel at work. The questions should be concise and have a friendly tone. Include both multiple-choice and open-ended questions that cover work environment, professional growth, perception of leadership, and work-life balance.
STAGE 6

Offboarding

Use prompts to support structured exits and respectful departure communication.

11

Exit checklist

Create a more complete offboarding checklist with placeholders for employee details and key actions.

Develop a customized employee offboarding checklist with space for inserting the employee’s name, department, and last workday. Include activities such as collection of company property, notifications to relevant departments, records updates, disabling accounts, and final pay/benefits instructions.
12

Exit announcement

Draft a respectful company-wide message that acknowledges contribution and future wishes.

Compose a company-wide email to announce an employee’s departure. Use a respectful and upbeat tone and include farewell wishes that show appreciation for the employee’s contributions and support for their next steps.

WHAT CHATGPT CANNOT REPLACE

Some HR work still depends on human judgment, context, and trust

The article says ChatGPT is not a substitute for human judgment, empathy, or strategic thinking. It specifically names complex employee relations, strategy and change, culture shaping, and leadership development and succession planning as areas where human involvement remains essential. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18}

CAN’T REPLACE 1

Complex employee relations

Conflict, misconduct, bias awareness, and sensitive employee issues still require empathy, discretion, and ethical oversight. :contentReference[oaicite:19]{index=19}

CAN’T REPLACE 2

Strategy and change leadership

AI can support outlines or communications, but it cannot fully create and implement strategy or lead change with human trust. :contentReference[oaicite:20]{index=20}

CAN’T REPLACE 3

Organizational culture

Culture still depends on human influence, ownership, alignment, and accountability across managers and teams. :contentReference[oaicite:21]{index=21}

CAN’T REPLACE 4

Leadership development and succession

Potential, motivation, long-term fit, and coaching judgment go beyond what a text model can truly assess. :contentReference[oaicite:22]{index=22}

  • Emotional nuance
  • Ethical judgment in human situations
  • Authentic trust-building relationships

Where HR still leads

  • Strategic choices balancing people and business needs
LIMITATIONS AND RISKS

What HR needs to watch out for

The guide highlights several limitations: hallucinations, biased answers, lack of source transparency, and outdated information. It says these limitations can lead to real-world risks such as biased decisions, inaccurate HR guidance, and data privacy or cybersecurity exposure if they are not managed well. :contentReference[oaicite:23]{index=23}

LIMIT 1

Hallucinations

ChatGPT can produce confident-sounding answers that are still factually wrong. :contentReference[oaicite:24]{index=24}

LIMIT 2

Bias

The model can reflect biased patterns in training data and produce biased or skewed outputs. :contentReference[oaicite:25]{index=25}

LIMIT 3

Lack of source transparency

Outputs may not include reliable references, and cited sources can be fabricated or misattributed. :contentReference[oaicite:26]{index=26}

LIMIT 4

Outdated information

Without current grounding or verification, policy, legal, or best-practice guidance may be stale. :contentReference[oaicite:27]{index=27}

RISK 1

Bias in decision-making

If unchecked, AI use can reinforce unfair outcomes in HR processes. :contentReference[oaicite:28]{index=28}

RISK 2

Inaccurate HR guidance

Incorrect outputs can lead to poor decisions, compliance issues, or legal exposure. :contentReference[oaicite:29]{index=29}

RISK 3

Data privacy and cybersecurity

Employees may enter confidential information without oversight unless clear usage policies exist. :contentReference[oaicite:30]{index=30}

RESPONSIBLE USE

1

Make a plan

2

Test drive the tool

3

Do due diligence

4

Avoid sharing sensitive information

5

Be transparent

Create a policy

7

Get professional advice
